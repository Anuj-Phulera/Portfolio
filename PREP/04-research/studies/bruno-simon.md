# bruno-simon.com — the portfolio as a place

**Fetched:** 2026-10-21 · **Author:** Bruno Simon · **Type:** WebGL portfolio

## What it is
A portfolio rendered as a three-dimensional world you drive through. The fetched document contains almost no text: a prompt to interact, a "stuck" affordance, and a link to a map.

## What it does
- The visitor operates a vehicle inside the site rather than scrolling a document.
- Content is discovered spatially — projects are objects in the world, not cards in a list.
- The "Unstuck" control is the most important UI element on the page: it admits that a 3D interface can trap people.
- A map link provides an alternative overview for anyone who does not want to drive.

## Why it works
It converts browsing into a memory. A visitor does not remember the third card in a grid; they remember the time they drove into a giant mailbox. Spatial memory is stronger than list memory, and it makes the author unforgettable in a way that a better-looking grid cannot.

## What it costs
- **Content is gated by competence.** A recruiter with ninety seconds and a trackpad learns less than they would from a plain list.
- **Device risk is severe.** Low-end phones, reduced-motion users, and keyboard-only users need a completely different experience.
- **It is a one-trick form.** It works because it is rare. It cannot be repeated across projects, and it does not suit a body of work that needs to be read.
- **SEO and copyability suffer.** Text inside a canvas is not text.

## What transfers
- **Spatial memory is the strongest recall mechanism available on the web.** If the portfolio uses space, it must be for the one thing worth remembering.
- **An escape hatch is part of the design, not an admission of failure.** "Unstuck" is exemplary.
- **Provide a second, boring path** for people who cannot or will not play.

## What must not transfer
The driving metaphor, the specific world, the palette, the vehicle. The lesson is *place*, not *car*.
