# craigmod.com — the personal site as a publication

**Fetched:** 2026-10-21 · **Author:** Craig Mod · **Type:** writer/photographer's site

## What it is
A personal site built around books, essays, photography and two newsletters, with a long, specific about section.

## What it does
- The homepage is a **catalogue of authored objects**: books with print specifications (240 pp., 56 colour plates, second printing), essays with publications named, photographs of physical editions.
- Writing is the interface. There is no interaction gimmick; the page is typographically calm and information-dense.
- Specificity is used as proof: print runs, paper stocks, which edition, which publisher, which year.
- A single personal line — *"the only thing that matters is the quality and care of work you put out into the world"* — sits inside a list of credentials, not above it.

## Why it works
The site is unmistakably one person's, because no one else could have this list. It persuades through **irreproducible specificity** — not "I love design," but "sixth printing, printed and bound in Japan." A visitor believes the work exists because the details could only come from having done it.

## What it costs
- Almost no motion; a visitor looking for spectacle will leave.
- The density assumes readerly patience.
- It leans on an established reputation; a student version would need the specificity to do more work.

## What transfers
- **Specificity is the cheapest form of credibility.** "Third revision, after the pinned-scroll version failed on mobile" is worth more than any adjective.
- **List objects, not skills.**
- **Put the personal line inside the evidence, not above it.**

## What must not transfer
The layout, the typefaces, the newsletter funnel, the particular voice.
