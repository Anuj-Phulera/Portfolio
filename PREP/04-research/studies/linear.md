# linear.app — explaining a product by performing it

**Fetched:** 2026-10-21 · **Type:** product marketing site

## What it is
The marketing site for Linear, a product-development tool. The page is built from interface fragments: issue lists, activity feeds, a roadmap, an AI agent transcript.

## What it does
- The hero states the category in eleven words, then immediately shows the product's own material rather than an abstract illustration.
- Sections are named like interface regions: *Intake and integrations*, *Planning and monitoring*, *AI and automations* — each with a live-looking specimen of the UI.
- Figures are labelled (`Fig 0.1`, `Fig 0.2`) — a small editorial decision that makes marketing read as documentation.
- Motion is used to demonstrate state change (an issue moving from Todo to In Progress), not to decorate the page.

## Why it works
A visitor learns what the product *does* by watching it act. The site never says "powerful"; it shows a workflow and lets the reader infer power. It also borrows the credibility of technical documentation through typography and figure numbering.

## What it costs
- Extremely expensive to maintain: every marketing claim must be re-rendered as a convincing specimen.
- Dense and fast; a slower reader may feel behind.
- The aesthetic is now widely imitated and reads as "startup default" when executed without the same precision.

## What transfers
- **Show the artefact, not an illustration of the artefact.**
- **Label things like documentation** — a figure number, a caption, a source line. It changes the register from advertisement to evidence.
- **Animate state change only.** Motion that depicts a change in state carries meaning; motion that merely moves does not.

## What must not transfer
The Linear look: the specific gradient meshes, the pill buttons, the exact type scale, the dark-to-light section rhythm.
