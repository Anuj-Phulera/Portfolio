# neal.fun — the index of self-contained toys

**Fetched:** 2026-10-21 · **Author:** Neal Agarwal · **Type:** collection of interactive pieces

## What it is
A personal index of dozens of self-contained interactive experiments — *The Password Game*, *Spend Elon's Money*, *The Deep Sea*, *Infinite Craft*, *Draw a Perfect Circle*.

## What it does
- The homepage is a grid of illustrated links. Each project has its own visual world and its own interaction; nothing is templated beyond the link itself.
- A tiny live detail (a plant that reports how many times it has been watered today) gives the index a pulse.
- The range is the point: scale, satire, education, toys. No unified "brand system" beyond the author's humour.

## Why it works
Every link promises a different kind of surprise, so the visitor keeps clicking — not because the page is beautiful, but because each item is *legibly a thing you can do*. The index's job is to make the next click obvious.

## What it costs
- No coherence: a visitor who wants to understand the author as a professional learns little.
- Accessibility and mobile behaviour vary enormously across pieces.
- The approach depends on volume and novelty; it cannot carry a career narrative.

## What transfers
- **One idea per project, legible in the link.**
- **A live, small, honest detail** — something that changes — makes a static index feel inhabited.
- **The index should advertise verbs, not nouns.** "Draw a perfect circle" beats "Interactive canvas experiment."

## What must not transfer
The cartoon illustration style, the grid, the specific toys.
