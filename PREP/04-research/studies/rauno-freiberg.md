# rauno.me — restraint as a display of skill

**Fetched:** 2026-10-21 · **Author:** Rauno Freiberg · **Type:** personal site

## What it is
A single page, mostly type, by an interaction designer who works with Vercel. It links to Devouring Details, Craft, a history of software design, projects and notes.

## What it does
- The entire visible page is a sentence about the author and six imperative lines: *Make it fast. Make it beautiful. Make it consistent. Make it carefully. Make it timeless. Make it soulful. Make it.*
- No hero image, no animation reel, no case-study cards.
- Navigation is typographic, not iconographic. Email is a copy interaction with a state change ("Copied").
- The site's argument is made by the site's finish, not by a claim about finish.

## Why it works
At this level of reduction there is nowhere to hide. Spacing, kerning, the exact response of a link, the behaviour of a copied state — all of it is exposed. The page says "I care about details" and simultaneously demonstrates it, which is the only way that claim is ever credible.

## What it costs
- It assumes the visitor already knows who the author is, or will click through. A cold visitor learns almost nothing about the work from the landing page.
- It offers no narrative and no emotional arc. It is a door, not a room.
- It only works when the craft is exceptional; a mediocre version of this page would read as empty.

## What transfers
**Let the finish carry the claim.** If the portfolio says "I care about detail," every millisecond of transition and every focus ring must agree. One sloppy state destroys the argument.

**A single typographic system can be an identity.** No logo required.

## What must not transfer
The layout, the six-line manifesto, the particular austerity. Copying the surface of a minimalist site produces an empty site, not a rigorous one.
