# pudding.cool — the visual essay as a form

**Fetched:** 2026-10-21 · **Publisher:** The Pudding · **Type:** data journalism

## What it is
A digital publication of visual essays, each numbered, with categories for favourites, popular, updating, reader input, video and audio.

## What it does
- Every piece is an **argument with a visual method**, not a dashboard. Titles are questions or provocations: *Why some people mow a lawn better than others*; *What do America's earliest restaurant menus teach us about America?*
- Content is organised by editorial type (video, audio, interactive, reader input) rather than by topic alone.
- Small physical-object illustrations (stickers) carry personality without competing with the data.
- The archive is deep and consistent: the same publication grammar across hundreds of pieces.

## Why it works
The reader knows within one screen what kind of thing they are in for: a story with evidence. The visual system is consistent enough to be recognisable and loose enough to let each essay have its own form. That is the opposite of a template — a **grammar**, not a layout.

## What it costs
- Each piece is bespoke and expensive. This does not scale to a solo portfolio with eight projects.
- Heavy interactivity carries real accessibility risk; a chart you must drag is a chart some people cannot read.
- The playful illustration style is specific to their voice and would be borrowed identity if copied.

## What transfers
- **Format as identity.** "Numbered visual essay" is a container that makes disparate subjects cohere.
- **A consistent frame, a different form inside it.** This is how a portfolio can hold eight unlike projects without forcing them into one template.
- **Ask a question in the title.** It converts a topic into a reason to keep reading.

## What must not transfer
Sticker illustrations, the exact grid, the numbering conceit, the colours.
