Scores are judgments, not measurements. Scale 1–5, where 5 is strongest. Cost is scored so that **5 = cheapest to build**.

## Reading

- **A is the safest and the least memorable.** It wins on trust and loses on pulse. Its ideas (receipts, status stamps) are portable into any other direction.
- **B is the most emotional and the most fragile.** It depends entirely on the quality of five short texts and on lighting craft. It also carries the highest accessibility and performance debt.
- **C is the most aligned with the stated goal** and the most expensive. It is also the only direction in which the visitor *experiences* the author's engineering rather than reading about it.

## Recombinable parts

These are separable and should be evaluated individually, not as a package:

1. **Receipts** (A) — attach to any direction.
2. **Status stamps** (A) — attach to any direction.
3. **Held frames and cuts** (B) — a pacing rule, applicable everywhere.
4. **Optional room tone** (B) — a sound policy, applicable everywhere.
5. **Operable miniatures** (C) — the core of C only.
6. **Lab notes / the back of the object** (C) — attach to any direction.

## The honest recommendation (not a decision)

**C as the spine, with A's receipt discipline and B's pacing.**

Reason: the north star is about what the subject can *create next*, and operation is the only mechanism that produces that inference directly. A's receipt layer prevents C from becoming a toy by making every instrument traceable. B's rule — hold the frame, cut rather than fade — keeps C from becoming a busy dashboard.

**Why this recommendation could be wrong:**
- C may be unbuildable to the required standard in the available time, and a half-built bench is worse than a finished ledger.
- The subject may not want his work reduced to instruments; some projects (RAKSHABANDHAN) are narratives, not machines.
- A hybrid can become mush. If we recombine, one direction must remain dominant and the others must appear as *rules*, not as visual styles.

**What must happen before a choice:** the subject reviews all three and states which one he would be proud to defend, and which one he would be embarrassed by. That second answer is usually the more informative one.
