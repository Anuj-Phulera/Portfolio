# The middle — how the work is sequenced

The middle is where most portfolios die: a grid of projects, each the same shape, each less interesting than the last. The fix is editorial, not visual.

## Sequence principle

**Alternate proof and doubt.** Never place two triumphant projects in a row; never place two confessions in a row. The rhythm is: *something built → what it cost → something built differently → what it taught.*

## Proposed order (subject to consent and correction)

| # | Subject | Mode of presentation | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **STRATA** | Operable instrument (Direction C) or a documented entry (A) | Strongest, most defensible; establishes the thesis immediately |
| 2 | **VERDE** | Two-person build, split credit, hardware/software | Proves collaboration and range right after the solo thesis |
| 3 | **The pattern** | A short interstitial: the audit finding — countable claims exact, experiential claims aspirational | The confession that earns everything after it |
| 4 | **SKILLVERSE** | Data exhibit, aggregate-only, methodology visible | Proves research rigour |
| 5 | **NIRMAAN** | Architecture and argument; withheld branding | Shows product thinking and the courage to withdraw work |
| 6 | **RESONATE** | The unshipped one — shaders and the reason it never deployed | Proves honesty and visual craft |
| 7 | **RAKSHABANDHAN** | Only with consent; otherwise cited as the source of the "missing data" principle, without private detail | Emotional centre, or its principle |
| 8 | **The next thing** | A single unbuilt idea, described precisely | Answers "what could he create next" |

## Interstitials — the connective tissue

Three short passages, each under 80 words, each doing one job:

1. **After STRATA:** what the interface is allowed to do, and why permission matters.
2. **After the pattern:** the exact sentence — *the legend is written first; the proof arrives later* — followed by the correction.
3. **Before the ending:** one paragraph on what is deliberately not shown, and why (consent, unfinished work, unaudited claims).

Interstitials are typographic. No imagery, no animation beyond a fade. They are the rests between movements.

## Per-project page grammar (not a template)

Every project page answers the same **questions**, in a different **form**:

1. What was the problem? *(a sentence, not a paragraph)*
2. What did you build? *(the artefact — operable, recorded, or quoted in source)*
3. What is actually true about it? *(status + evidence)*
4. Who else built it? *(attribution, before features)*
5. What did it cost? *(the failure, the compromise, the thing that broke)*
6. What would you do next? *(one concrete answer)*

The form varies: an instrument, a dossier, a timeline, a source excerpt. The questions do not.

## The scroll contract

- No page requires more than 4 viewport heights before the first substantive fact.
- No page hides the status stamp below the fold.
- No page auto-plays media.
- Every page ends with a link to the next project, chosen by the author, not by a "next in grid" rule.

## The failure test

Read the sequence with all visuals removed, as plain text. If it is boring, no amount of motion will save it. If it is compelling, the visuals have something to carry.
