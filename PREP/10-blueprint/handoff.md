## What is proven

- The subject's countable claims are accurate (34 routes, 35 nodes, 79 edges, 262 rows, 245 lines).
- The drift is in experiential claims, and it has a documented correction: *countable claims exact, experiential claims aspirational.*
- Two credibility blockers are concrete and cheap to fix: generated mockups presented as screenshots; unlicensed avatars.
- A genuine design idea already exists in the work: rendering missing data as missing.
- A working precedent for trustworthy adaptation already exists: STRATA's five constraints.

## What is not proven

- Anything about deployed sites (no browser, restricted network).
- The subject's stage, audience, or final direction (intentionally unanswered).
- Whether any of the three directions can be built to standard in the available time.
- Whether the reduced-motion and accessibility plans work in practice.

## The first three actions after handoff

1. The subject answers Q1–Q4 in `open-questions.md` and records them in `decision-log.md`.
2. A browser-equipped machine runs the three demo pages and captures the real state of each source project.
3. Stage 1 of the roadmap begins only when `readiness-criteria.md` is fully checked.

## The one thing to protect

The portfolio's advantage is not visual. It is that everything on it can be checked. Any future decision that trades verifiability for spectacle reverses the only defensible position this project has.
