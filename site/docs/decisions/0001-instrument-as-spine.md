# 0001 — The instrument is the spine, the receipt is the check

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-22

## Context

Three directions were prepared and none was approved. The comparison scored
Direction C (the Instrument) highest on originality, proof of capability and the
north-star test, and lowest on cost. It also recommended, explicitly, a hybrid:
*C as the spine, A's receipt discipline, B's pacing* — with the warning that a
hybrid becomes mush unless one direction stays dominant and the others appear
as rules rather than styles.

## Decision

Build C. Take two things from the others and nothing else:

- **From A:** every claim is openable, and every entry carries a status stamp.
- **From B:** hold before revealing, cut between sections, never animate type
  word by word.

The bench is the visual world: warm off-white, graphite, hairlines, one signal
colour used for state. No dark theme, no glow, no gradient, no particles. If a
borrowed idea needs a new visual language to work, it is not borrowed — it is a
second direction, and it was rejected.

## Consequences

- The site must be operable, not merely readable, which means the instruments
  have to work. Three shipped; the fourth is the cloth on the home page.
- Every instrument needs a lab note, because a reduction that does not admit
  what it removed is a toy.
- The cost estimate in the comparison was right. This is the most expensive of
  the three directions, and the three instruments are why.

## What would reverse this

If the instruments read as gimmicks to three people who do not know the work,
the bench has failed on its own terms and Direction A is the fallback — the
receipts and stamps already exist and would survive the change.
